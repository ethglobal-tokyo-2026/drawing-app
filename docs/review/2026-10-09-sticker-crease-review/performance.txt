Sticker crease: performance review (2026-10-09, origin/main at 95a45b14)

Board under test: crease-ann, 32 own stickers on the phone layout (U = 390 px, s 0.30-0.36, so long sides
117-140 CSS px). 28 crease bakes, 6 of them empty, so 22 creases on screen, 88 crease layers (<i>).

PART 1: RISKS, RANKED (computed from the code; "unverified" = not measured here)

1. The moving light restyles every crease layer and restarts its fade, every write.
   light.ts writes --lx/--ly on each .sticker-crease at most every 45 ms while the light moves. Each
   write re-resolves the calc/hypot chain on 4 <i> per crease (88 here) and, through
   `transition: opacity 280ms`, cancels and restarts up to 88 opacity transitions. A running opacity
   transition promotes its element to its own compositing layer in Chromium and WebKit, so a moving
   light keeps up to 88 extra layers alive, and everything above them that overlaps (resin, sweep,
   veil, the stickers stacked higher) may need layers too (overlap promotion). Measured in Part 2.
   Fixes: drop the fade (no-fade), or write the light to creases only once it settles (settle), or
   collapse the 4 layers into fewer (see 5).

2. Layer memory on iOS while the light moves (unverified on a device). One <i> layer at DPR 3 for a
   109x140 sticker is 327x420 device px = 0.55 MB; 88 of them = ~48 MB of backing store, before
   overlap-promoted layers. iPad (DPR 2, large layout stickers 1.25x): ~0.38 MB each. WebKit's web
   content process is jetsam-killed on memory pressure inside LINE. Fix: same as 1; fewer layers per
   crease (5).

3. The worker's mask cache never lets go. creaseWorker.ts keeps every mask URL's ImageBitmap (up to
   320 px a side: a 549x704 cut is 250x320 = 0.32 MB) for the worker's life, across boards. ~35 masks
   per board here = ~11 MB; every other person's board visited adds theirs (30 boards ~ 300 MB,
   unverified extrapolation). Fix: an LRU cap (a board's worth, ~64 masks), or clear when no board
   holds the worker; or keep alpha only as a Uint8Array at bake size (1 B/px, not 4).

4. Bake cost grows with the pile, quadratic in the worst case. Per job: draw and getImageData each
   sticker underneath (k reads of w*h*4), then stackedSurface blurs a full-size copy once per layer
   above the first (k-1 blurs of 6 sliding passes each), then drape (4 envelope passes + blur) and 4
   PNG encodes. A pile where every sticker overlaps every other costs O(n^2) draws and blurs. At 32
   stickers it's small (Part 2: whole board 120 ms Chromium, 870 ms WebKit on this Mac; an iPhone
   maybe 2-4x WebKit's, unverified). Re-bakes: every board mount (the store is per mount, so every
   visit to someone else's board and every remount of your own); picking a sticker up re-bakes every
   neighbor without it, and the drop re-bakes them again; on someone else's board, selecting moves the
   sticker to the top, which re-bakes it and what it now covers. iPad: stickers land 1.25x, so ~1.56x
   the pixels per bake (DPR doesn't matter: scale is capped at 1). Fix if piles grow: cache bakes by
   key across mounts (a module-level LRU of blobs), and cap k to the nearest few underneath.

5. Four images per crease where two would do (design-level fix for 1 and 2). The blend weights are
   clamp(a), clamp(-a), clamp(b), clamp(-b): at most two sides show at once. Two layers per crease
   (lit-from-a and lit-from-b, signed) or one canvas per board would halve layers and decoded images.
   Decoded images today: 4 x 109x140x4 B = 0.25 MB per crease, ~5.4 MB for this board.

6. Landing creases one at a time. Each worker reply lands alone: 4 createObjectURL, a React commit of
   that one sticker, a style/paint of the board and 4 PNG decodes, 22 times over the bake span. Fine
   at 32 stickers (unverified as a jank source); batch landings per frame if bakes get faster than
   frames.

Low risk, checked: useCreases per render builds a signature string only (cheap); creaseJobs (n^2/2
SAT overlap tests, JSON keys) runs only when the signature changes, and a drag moves the sticker by
writing its transform directly (useBoardGestures.ts), not through React state, so it doesn't run per
drag frame. Object URLs are revoked on every replace and on unmount.

Reading:
- The known cost reproduces in Chromium: current spends 2-2.5x off's main-thread time while the light
  moves, about 3 s more of it in style recalc, and drops to 36-38 fps at 4x.
- no-fade is a regression on WebKit, the iPhone's engine: 13-15 fps, frames of 150+ ms. With the fade,
  each write starts composited opacity animations; without it, each write changes the opacity of 88
  painted layers, which WebKit repaints on the main thread at DPR 3. Rejected.
- settle matches off in both engines (WebKit 59.3 vs 59.5 fps; Chromium 4x main 4.8-5.6 s vs off
  3.7-5.1 s), but the creases hold still while the light moves and catch up 250 ms after it stops.

Chromium's current spread (19.7 to 54.9 fps across rounds) follows the host's load: CPU throttling is

New finding, WebKit only: a 120-260 ms frame after the light stops, with creases on, in every fading
variant; with creases off the tail's longest frame is 22-24 ms. It lands as the fades end (settle's
comes after its catch-up). Likely WebKit tearing down the fades' compositing layers and repainting the
creases at DPR 3 (mechanism unverified). On an iPhone that's a hitch each time the light comes to
rest, unverified on a device. settle doesn't remove it; fewer crease layers (2 per crease, risk 5) or
painting the blend without opacity animations would.

VERDICT
settle gets closest to off while still following the light: frames during a moving light match off in
both engines, and the crease follows the light 250 ms after it rests, gliding on the existing fade.
beat keeps following during the move but only helps Chromium (about half of current's extra cost);
on WebKit it's no better than current. no-fade is rejected (WebKit 13-15 fps). Next, for the
WebKit rest hitch and layer memory: two layers per crease instead of four.
