# Real-surface light: a measured physical model

**Goal (ad0ll):** "an accurate simulation that looks really, really visually striking." Every value is measured or computed from a source. Where it isn't, it's labeled a design decision, with its reason.

- **Live** (9ffdce3d): one soft ceiling light, steeper creases, paper light, drop shadows, foil lit by the one light only, shadow softness from the light, and the crease step's contact shadow.
- **Research notes:** `data/scratch/physical-model/`.

## Decisions for ad0ll

1. **The light's reflection at its real brightness.** Physically it's about 5× paper white, and a screen can't show that, so the print under it turns white. Real stickers do the same in a band of tilts.
   - Or cap the reflection near half of paper white (a design decision): the print stays faintly readable under it (about 1.9:1 instead of about 1.03:1), but it no longer reads as a light.
   - Mock (draft branch `mock-coat-light`): `data/scratch/mocks/coat-reflection/reflection-webkit.png`. At real brightness the light reads as a light only with R2's sharp edge and CIE veil; with today's soft edge and halo it reads as bloom. Capped reads as fog, or with the sharp edge as a dim lamp.
   - The patch is as big as a real one: about 570 CSS px across on a phone, wider than its screen, and 720 on an iPad. Wherever it reflects, that part of the board blanks until you tilt.
   - If physical: the still pose's glare sits off the stickers, so a device that never moves never hides one; and foil under the light brightens to the metal's reflection of it, since foil sees it far dimmer today.
   - Recommended: physical.
2. **How much the coat deepens the art's colors (δ).**
   - At 1, physical: mid-tones deepen and colors saturate, the gloss look. Paper white and black stay put.
   - At 0, colors stay as drawn: printed as if for the laminated stock, also physical.
   - Mock: `data/scratch/mocks/coat-reflection/coat-darkening-webkit.png`. On screen, under lit paper #EBEBEB, gray #BCBCBC shows #8F8F8F at 1, #A1A1A1 at 0.5 and #AEAEAE today; skin #F0C8A8 shows #D19C7C, #D8AD8E and #DEB99B.
   - Recommended: 1, the gloss look; 0.5 if drawn grays darkening by about a third is too far from what artists picked.
3. **The board's physical scale.** At life size, a sticker on the board is a small real sticker (about 1.9 cm on an iPhone), and creases are drawn at real thickness and drape. Miniature makes them sub-pixel.
   - Recommended: life size.
4. **Re-centering at extreme poses.**
   - Today the viewer re-centers within a second of any turn past 25° (`RECENTER_RELEASE`, `RECENTER_MS`). So a held extreme pose (far left, right, forward or back) loses the grazing view's contrast: at β 120, crease contrast goes from −40% while moving to −14% once held.
   - When you tilt to look, your head hasn't moved. When you lie down, it has. Without face tracking, only how long a pose lasts tells them apart.
   - iOS 26's Liquid Glass light, the closest shipped match, re-centers once the device stays past 12.5° for 10 s or past 25° for 5 s, with a 1.5 s fade, and the timer restarts when the device comes back inside. These are its undocumented defaults, read from its system framework (`LightSourceSupport`). UIKit's parallax drifts back continuously instead, which would slide a held highlight away.
   - Recommended: Liquid Glass's two tiers and a 1.5 s ease, measured over the whole turn, since lying on your side turns the phone within the screen's plane. A look that comes back never re-centers.
   - Never re-centering is fully physical, but in bed the ceiling light sits behind the phone and the laminate goes dead; turning in a chair or handing the phone over leaves the light somewhere arbitrary; and the heading drifts without a compass.
   - Research notes: `data/scratch/physical-model/recentering.txt`.

## Build, after the decisions (each lane owns its files)

- **R1, the room's bounce:**
  - a generator that solves and bakes the room, in the way `generateFoilLookup.ts` makes its basis;
  - gloss and foil read it with a reflection ray against the box;
  - creases take its spherical harmonics;
  - `glossBounce`, `foilBounce` and `roomBounce` retire.
- **R2, the light:** brightness from lumens; CIE halo; 0.5° edge; foreshortened by sin e; fixed in the room. Eye distance and still pose by device class.
- **R3, paper and coat:** interfaced Lambertian with δ; gloss blur 0.024; n 1.51; PBR Neutral highlight compression.
- **R4, creases:** the S-curve at real thickness, L about 2 mm, symmetric tones, `CREASE_STEEPENING` gone, contact-shadow cap raised. Today's steepened slope (7.3°) already matches a 2 mm drape over a 0.175 mm sticker, so the shading's strength stays. At extreme poses, the depth comes from decision 4, from long shadows at raking light, and from R1's room lighting creases when the light is behind the screen.
- **R5, motion:** re-centering by how long a pose lasts (decision 4), counted whether the device moves or holds, so fidgeting after lying down still re-centers; the 15° turn left over at β 135.
- **R6:** the physical-model table moves into `docs/research/sticker-laminate/README.md`, with sources, and tests follow each change.
- **Checks before deploy:**
  - every light, crease, laminate, foil and shadow e2e in both engines;
  - the holo harness;
  - a critique on the 21-sticker board;
  - ad0ll's device check in LINE.

## Open

- **Bright paper upright on ad0ll's iPad:** not reproduced in WebKit, where everything darkens there.
  - It could be the panel itself (OLEDs are brightest seen square-on, so facing the iPad squarely brightens everything on it), the real room's reflection in the iPad's glass, or LINE reporting the iPad's rotation differently.
  - It needs a screenshot at that pose, and a recorder run (developer slip) while tilting. A screenshot tells the two apart, since it shows only what the app drew, never the glass's glare.
- **Each foil's metal and the room's bounce** (ad0ll: after this): gold warms it, silver keeps it neutral, rose gold tints it, holo diffracts it. R1's solve gives the bounce to color.
- **Static shadows left:** the photo sticker, gift bag, give sheet pick, gift received notice and receive dialog. The tray is unchecked.
- **Cost, if the device check shows a problem:** cache each sticker's layout between publishes, and skip the work after the copies when only the light changed.
- **HDR output:** an HDR canvas (WebGPU in Safari 26) could show the reflection at real brightness. Unverified in LINE; deferred.

## Platform limits

- **Android (Chromium, and LINE's Android WebView):** `deviceorientation` fires only after a 0.1° change, so slow tilts step. iOS has no threshold.
- **LINE's in-app browser on iPad:** the screen angle is unverified on a device.
