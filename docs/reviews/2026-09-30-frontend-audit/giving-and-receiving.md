## Audit (technical)

Proposed scores for this slice (0–4):

- **A11y 2**: the receive dialog is well built (modal, labelled, a slider with arrow keys plus Enter/Space/End, alerts that persist), but the give, offer and Send gratitude dialogs are not modal for screen readers, focus falls to `<body>` whenever a busy key disables, the selected offer row fails AA, and a 2.4 s hop repeats forever on the home screen.
- **Performance 3**: no long frames measured in the pull (0 frames over 33 ms even with the CPU throttled 4×), one backdrop-filter in the bag, transform and opacity animations; per-frame React state in the pull and two ~390 kB hero images for LINE are the headroom left.
- **Theming 3**: tokens are used throughout (`--aqua`, `--rule`, `--r-label`, the easings); drift is small and specific: white 10px text on coded fields, off-scale sizes and weights, raw z-indexes.
- **Responsive 2**: the main flows fit at 375×591, but every sheet that grows with content (the sticker picker, the offer swap state, the bag view) pushes its primary key below a fold with a hidden scrollbar, and the offer confirmation draws its sticker at natural size.
- **Integrity 2**: the offer sheet reports "Offer sent" and promises a LINE reply while sending nothing, chain errors reach people as English developer strings, and the giver's notice replays every past receive on a new device; the give and receive logic underneath is careful (recoverable, retried, never moves a sticker on unmount).

### Positives (what to keep)

- The receive dialog's semantics: `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal`, a name from the giver, a slider named "Pull the tab to open the gift" that takes arrow keys (two presses read `aria-valuenow` 40, five open it) plus Enter, Space and End, focus landing on the slider and then on Accept, and failures kept in the sheet as `role="alert"` instead of toasts. ARIA snapshot: `dialog "A gift from @Giving-a" › heading › slider › paragraph`.
- Every non-drag way to open the bag worked when tried: double-tap and press-and-hold each showed the Accept sheet in about 1.28 s (`b2` `doubleTapOpens: 1285`, `holdOpens: 1284`); a 315 px drag opened it in 1.7 s (`b3b` `drag315px`). With reduced motion there is no hint or finger, and the Accept sheet is up 23 ms after the snap (`reduced-acceptMs: 23`).
- Careful flow logic underneath: a packed gift is recoverable after a reload (`giftBackend.ts:151-202`), the deposit and take-out are retried and keep their transaction hash, unmounting never moves a sticker (`giveFlow.ts:239-244`), and every step's failure is persisted in the sheet with its reason and which sticker it was.
- Rendering cost is disciplined: the hint animates transform and opacity only (the CSS comment at `GiftBag.css:732-735` says why), one backdrop-filter, and 0 frames over 33 ms in the measured drag even with the CPU 4× slower.
- The layouts hold at the tight size: at 375×591 the receive dialog's hint ends at y 577, the Accept sheet is 299 px and the sticker sits above it without overlap, and the giver's notice fits with 8 px spare (`b2` `se-accept`, `notice375`).
- Touch targets: header icon buttons are 38 px with a 3 px pseudo-element (44 px), the pull slider's hit box measured 74×50, quiet links carry their bands.

## Design review (Assessment A)

### Design specificity verdict

Authored for Croquis, and at its best unmistakably so. The gift bag is a whole object: a frosted konbini sleeve, aqua tear tape printed with the seal date, a luggage tag with the giver's handle, rubber stamps (Opened, Taken back, 1:1 chat), a torn-open reveal in which the foil-edged sticker rises out, and on the giver's side a hatched silhouette where the sticker used to be. Nothing about it fits a generic wallet or a gacha shop. The parts that could belong to any app are the ones with no material of their own: the offer sheet's three radio rows and amount chips, the sticker picker (a plain grid of small thumbnails labelled by number), and the give sheet's single list row with a caret.

### Heuristics

1. Visibility of system status: 3. The bag's packed, preparing, picking, sealed and "On its way" states are all drawn, but the longest wait has a grey key and no progress, and the offer confirmation reports a status that is false.
2. Match between system and real world: 3. The sleeve, tag, tape and stamp are exact metaphors and the vocabulary is the project's; "Confirm in your wallet" and the chain error strings break it.

### Cognitive load

- Give sheet: one primary, one quiet link, one sentence. No failures.
- Picker: the choice is among many near-identical thumbnails labelled only by number, with no grouping, sort or search (fails "minimize choices" past about 12 stickers), and the key sits far from the choice (P2 above).
- Offer sheet: three modes in one sheet (ask, swap, offer gratitude), each revealing its own sub-control; the key's label never changes, so what pressing it means depends on a mode chosen a screen earlier. Decision points over four options: the picker only.
- The receiver's path is short: pull, Accept, then one question about Gratitude.

### Emotional journey

- Giver: calm start (the sticker large and dimmed, one row) → delight (the bag opens, the sticker drops in) → the valley: "Preparing your gift" with a grey key, a wallet sentence and no exit → LINE's own picker (out of the app's hands) → payoff (the bag seals; "Sealed and sent"; the board's spot goes blank and a film badge "On its way" appears) → afterglow when the receiver takes it (the notice: their picture beside the hatched hole, a heart in the title). Weak points: the valley, and the afterglow repeating for every past gift on a new device.
- Receiver: the link opens straight onto the sealed bag with a finger hint (curiosity) → the tape tears with ticks and a shiver (tactile) → the foil-edged sticker rises and a sheet says "This sticker is for you, <name>." (peak) → Accept → it flies to the board → one question, "Send <name> gratitude?". Reassurance at the stakes: Not now leaves the gift waiting, and the terms line says plainly that the giver will see their LINE name and picture. Weak point: the same line says "You agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy" while those pages are placeholders (PRODUCT.md).

### Strengths

1. The reveal is one continuous physical event, from pull to rising sticker to Accept, and it works by hand, by double-tap, by hold and by keyboard, so the metaphor is never the only way in.
2. The failure states are specific and recoverable: "Not sent yet" keeps the sticker in the bag with Send in LINE and Take it out, each refusal has its own bag and stamp and exactly one way on (own gift, already opened), and errors stay on the sheet.
3. The hatched silhouette where a given sticker used to be (on the giving screens and in the giver's notice) makes scarcity felt without a sentence, as PRODUCT.md asks.
