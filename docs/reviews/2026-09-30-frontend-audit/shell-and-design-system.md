## Audit (technical)

Proposed scores for this slice (0–4):

- **A11y 2** — careful primitives (named icons, busy keys, ~53 live regions, Space/Enter press), but the viewport blocks zoom app-wide, the shared Sheet isn't modal, NSFW marks are white on pink under 11px, and the consent line leads to a page that autoplays audio with no stop.
- **Performance 3** — transform/opacity motion throughout, no `transition: all`, no layout-property transitions or keyframes; `will-change` left on every board sticker; one backdrop blur, purposeful.
- **Theming 3** — a real token system and a disciplined Ink-alpha shadow vocabulary; drift: the z-index scale is barely used, ~38 font sizes sit between the type steps, the desktop frame and the NSFW marks bypass tokens and rules.
- **Responsive 3** — tabs hold in Japanese at 375 (measured); keys and labels meet 44px by construction; the grabber ignores the bottom safe area (unverified on device).
- **Integrity 3** — one press, one key per screen, one icon registry, honest skeletons; a dead duplicate toast, two focus-list implementations, joke legal pages behind a consent sentence.

## Design review (Assessment A)

### Design specificity verdict

Authored for Croquis. The keycap with its ink outline and deep lip, label stock on 3px of paper, perforation rows as a sheet's grab, index tabs cut from label stock that stick on, the "SEAL · シール" maker print, the hit counter's fighting-game speed lines, and a press that sinks, creeps, pops and fires 60ms into the pop: none of it would transfer unchanged to another product. The generic moments in this slice are the motion card (a platform permission prompt in a sheet, in platform words) and the desktop frame (a phone on grey, fine for people who opened a link on a computer). The legal pages are specific, in the wrong way.

### Heuristics (app shell and shared primitives)

1. Visibility of system status — **3** · skeletons with one status line, busy keys keep their face, tabs show current by fill and lift; failures that go through the toast vanish in 2.4s.
2. Match between system and real world — **4** · the trade-book materials hold everywhere (tear the perforation, press the key, stick the tab on); the lone mismatch is "grant permissions for motion controls".
3. User control and freedom — **3** · Back closes the top overlay app-wide, a slide-off cancels a press, Escape cancels a held press; the shared Sheet has no Escape or Back, and the privacy page's audio has no stop.
4. Consistency and standards — **3** · one press, one key per screen, one icon registry; drift in two toasts (one dead), two focus-list rules, a yellow ring on ink labels, ~38 off-scale type sizes, a z-index scale most code ignores.
5. Error prevention — **3** · the press cancels on slide-off and busy keys refuse a second press; the motion card's Allow fires even after a slide-off.
6. Recognition rather than recall — **4** · tabs carry icon and label; the grabber says "Board" until first used; "Make your first sticker" points at Draw.
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use — **2** · keyboard Space/Enter press and arrow keys exist, but zoom is off app-wide and the drawing screen's tabs take two steps (grabber, then tab).
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design — **4** · calm Liner and Ink, one coded key per screen, each hue with one meaning (01-board-en-390.png).
9. Recognize, diagnose, recover from errors — **3** · 27 `role="alert"` failure lines, most with reasons and a retry; copy failures only toast.
10. Help and documentation — **3** · the app teaches by doing; the one place a cautious person looks for policy (Terms, Privacy) holds jokes.
    **Total 32/40 (80%) — Good.**

### Cognitive load

Checklist failures: 0 of 8 for the shell (one key per screen, three tabs, two choices on the motion card). Watch item: hidden navigation on the drawing screen (tabs tucked behind a 36×5 bar), mitigated by the first-use "Board" pull tab. No decision point in this slice shows more than 4 options.

### Emotional journey

- **Peak:** the press. Keys sink with a slow creep, pop and then act; a tab sticks on with a 2px lift and a 1.04 settle. It makes every tap feel like handling stickers.
- **Valley, first open on iPhone:** before anything else, a sheet asks to "grant permissions for motion controls", then iOS asks again. The first act in the app is a permissions chore, with no payoff named.
- **Valley, the Accept sheet:** a first-timer who checks "Privacy Policy" before accepting their first gift gets a Rick Roll (en) or "Nice boat." (ja) that unmutes on their next tap, right where reassurance was needed.
- **Reassurance done well:** a busy key keeps its color while a spend is in flight (no grey flash), and Back closes overlays instead of leaving LINE.

### Strengths

1. **The press as a system** (`src/ui/press.ts`): one module for every key, label and tab, with travel by material, slide-off cancel, pop-then-fire, and a reduced-motion path that keeps the state change.
2. **Hierarchy by material:** Key vs LabelButton vs QuietLink makes "one key per screen" a construction rule, and the label's `--hit` bands give 44–46px touch targets for free.
3. **Tabs that take Japanese:** equal thirds, 6px padding and a 4px icon gap under 390px keep マイボード whole at 375 (measured 112px tabs, 75px label; 04-board-375x591.png).
