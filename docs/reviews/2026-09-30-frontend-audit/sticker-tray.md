## Audit (technical)

Proposed scores for this slice (0–4):

- **A11y 2**: names, roles, `aria-expanded`, a key alternative for every gesture but one, and reduced motion are all there; but the focus order runs through 26 stickers hidden behind the front sheet, the spread is nested buttons with a 1.03:1 focus ring, and nothing in the tray announces anything.
- **Performance 3**: the Zipper's loop sleeps at rest, no listener leaks, packing is trivial; but the closed tray downloads about 1 MB of images at board load, alongside the board's own stickers.
- **Theming 3**: tray colors and the pull's pink live in component CSS as hex (duplicating `--pink`, and not its DESIGN.md deep partner), and focus rings differ by part; otherwise tokens (`--liner-lift`, `--ink`, `--fs-fine`, the eases) are used.
- **Responsive 2**: fits 390×741 and Japanese; at 375×591 the front sheet's foot, the stack's dated edges and +N are clipped away; dated edges are 14–15px targets.
- **Integrity 3**: one authored system with real mechanics; but a race, a focus loss with a blink, a NEW pip that real arrivals never light, dead API surface and console-only errors.
  Total 13/20 (Acceptable).

### Positives (what to keep)

- The Zipper matches DESIGN.md's release rules: a 20% pull springs shut, 30% opens; a 100px pull in 40, 60 and 100ms (2500, 1667, 1000 px/s) opens, in 150 and 250ms doesn't; a tap toggles both ways (`04-gestures.cjs` "A zipper"; `06-targeted.cjs` "B flick speeds").
- It costs nothing at rest: 0 requestAnimationFrame calls in 3s; net window listeners 0 after three Explore↔board cycles; one `.tray` in the DOM after them (`09-perf.cjs`).
- Packing is cheap and stable: 0.6ms for 50 stickers, 1.5ms for 200, 3.8ms for 600 (Chromium, warm profile cache).
- Reduced motion keeps every state change: open lands within 14ms, a page turn within 116ms, a filter at once, the peel's curl tilt is gone (`curl` transform empty), no tug, no foil animation (`08-scenarios.cjs` "R reduced motion").
- Language: every tray string is translated and glossary-true; the tabs fit (すべて 34 of 46px, 自作 22, ギフト 34); switching language in Settings updated the tray without a reload (`08` "L language switch").
- Touch and legibility: the X is 31px with a 7px pad, tabs 50×44 with pads, the pull's hit area 54×116; the dates keep the 11px floor in the spread (11px measured) and read 4.6–5.0:1 at every depth (Graphite under the sheet shade, computed); Ink on the tab fills is 5.5–16.8:1.
- Gestures are complete and recoverable: peel to the board saves a placement, tap sticks on, dragging a board sticker to the edge opens the tray, lands it and shuts it after (`04` "G", "H"); the X, dragging back, closing and the spread all send a pulled sheet home on top of the stack; focus returns to the pull when the tray shuts on a focused sticker.

## Design review (Assessment A)

### Design specificity verdict

Authored for Croquis, and the most authored surface in the app: a canvas tray with a real Zipper drawn tooth by tooth, sheets with perforated grips, holes cut in the shape of the stickers that left, dated edges as an index, a sticker that curls off the sheet under the thumb. No other product would carry this. The risk is literal metaphor beating legibility: the given spot, the sheet stack's index and the pull-out are physical facts with no on-screen cue, and the tray's proudest states (the newest sheet, the blank spot) are its emptiest.

### Heuristics

1. Visibility of system status: **3** — NEW badges, holes, a landing shadow and a relaxing mouth all say what's happening; nothing is said to non-visual users, and NEW never lights for real arrivals.
2. Match between system and real world: **4** — sticker sheets, kiss-cut holes, a zipper, folder tabs: exact and consistent with the product's vocabulary; no crypto or money words.
3. User control and freedom: **3** — early release springs shut, one X, drag back, Escape and a board tap all send things home; a board tap closing the tray while a sheet is out over it is unexpected.
4. Consistency and standards: **3** — a bespoke idiom done consistently in look; five focus-ring styles, tabs that aren't tabs, 15px targets beside 44px ones.
5. Error prevention: **3** — slop thresholds (10px direction lock, 26px peel, 60px pull-free) prevent stray drags; a filter/page race and the +N pad overlapping an edge slip through.
6. Recognition rather than recall: **2** — paging, pulling a sheet out, tapping an edge and the blank spot are hidden gestures; only the pull tugs.
7. Flexibility and efficiency: **3** — tap to stick, drag to place, PageUp/PageDown, Enter on a sticker; no jump to a sticker by number or giver in an 8-sheet tray.

### Cognitive load

Checklist failures: **minimal choices** (the open tray offers the pull, three tabs, swipe paging, three dated edges, +N, peel or tap on a sticker, hole tap, blank tap and pull-out at once: more than 8 targets) and **one thing at a time** (a drag's first 10px decide between paging, peeling and pulling; the person must know which they want before they start). Passes: single focus, chunking (a sheet holds at most about 8 stickers), grouping (paper vs canvas), visual hierarchy, working memory, progressive disclosure (tabs hide until a gift arrives; +N folds deep sheets). 2 failures: moderate load. Decision point above 4 options: the open tray as a whole.

### Emotional journey

Peaks: the pull dragging down with a tick and buzz per tooth and the flop at the stop; a sticker curling off its sheet under the thumb and sticking with a settle; a hole that breathes in Ink when a board sticker is dragged home. Valleys: the tray opens on the newest sheet, often nearly empty (one sticker on sheet 6 of 6 in the 390×741 capture), and reaching an older sticker means paging; a given sticker's spot is blank paper, so the scarcity the product wants "felt" isn't visible; at 375×591 the sheet is cut off with no dates. High-stakes moments are elsewhere (giving); here the only irreversible-feeling act is putting a sticker back, and it is reversible. The end of a visit is the tray zipping shut over a board of stickers, which is a good last beat.

### Strengths

1. The peel: a sticker curls at the edge under the thumb (`rotate3d` tilt, then a lifted 11° flight), its shadow previews where it lands, and the board's own stick settle finishes it; the gesture reverses when a board sticker is dragged to the edge and the tray opens for it.
2. The Zipper as an object and as a control: parting teeth with a tick and haptic per tooth, a pull that flops toward the next pull, ArrowDown/ArrowUp/Escape/Enter/Space on a real button with `aria-expanded`, and an instant reduced-motion path.
3. The holes: a kiss-cut outline with the maker print, breathing in Ink when the sticker comes home, so the tray reads as a page that remembers what left it.
